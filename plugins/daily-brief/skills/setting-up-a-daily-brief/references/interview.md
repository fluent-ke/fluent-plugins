# The interview

**Ask these. Do not answer them yourself.**

One question at a time, waiting for each answer. A list gets terse replies; a single question gets
a useful ramble, and the specifics live in the ramble.

Write every answer into the instance's `interview.md` **in their words**. Their phrasing goes into
the contract nearly verbatim — it beats anything you would compose.

Budget 20 minutes. If they have five, ask 1, 4 and 6.

---

## Their work

1. **Tell me what you actually do day to day.** Let them talk. You are listening for what they are
   accountable for, who they answer to, and what a bad week looks like.

2. **What would make you late to a meeting because you were still reading it?**

3. **What did you read this week that mattered, and why?** Concrete beats abstract.

## Their watchlist — the highest-value question here

4. **Name the companies, regulators, people and products you watch.** Competitors, partners,
   acquirers, the ones that worry you, the one whose product launch would ruin your quarter.

   **Write down every name.** Then ask: *who else?* — twice. The names that come out third are
   usually the ones that matter.

   This is the single biggest quality lever in the system. A named company returns signal; an
   industry name returns noise.

5. **Who regulates you, and does their word change your roadmap?** In a regulated business this
   is usually the most valuable section in the brief. If they are not regulated, skip it.

## Their boundaries

6. **What is definitely not useful?** Funding rounds below a size, a whole geography, vendor
   marketing, anything they already get elsewhere. Ask for the number or the name — "small
   rounds" is not actionable, "under $10M" is.

7. **Which markets and currencies?** Stops the brief defaulting to US coverage.

8. **Is there anything commercially sensitive that must never appear**, even from public sources?
   Client names, deal names, figures.

9. **Does your organisation have a policy on what can go into an AI tool?** In regulated
   businesses this is real, and it becomes an explicit prohibition in the contract.

## Their sources

10. **Which sources do you already trust?** These become preferred sources.

11. **Any you find unreliable?** Becomes an explicit avoid.

12. **Do you rely on anything paywalled?** Say plainly that paywalled pages cannot be fetched and
    verified, so those items get dropped rather than summarised from a headline. Better they hear
    it now than notice a gap later.

## Their format

13. **How long, honestly?** Most say "short" and mean five to eight items.

14. **Do you want interpretation, or only what happened?**

15. **Phone or laptop?**

16. **What would make you forward this to your board or your team?** This sets the writing
    standard, and it is worth asking even if they have no board.

## Delivery

17. **What time, and which timezone?** Confirm explicitly. Never infer from where they live —
    people travel, and some work a Sunday-to-Thursday week.

18. **Which days?** Ask. Do not assume Monday to Friday.

19. **Where should it arrive** — email, or waiting in Claude? If email, **ask them for the
    address.** Never take it from a directory, roster or signature block.

20. **Anyone else receiving it?** Confirm each address with them, and tell them the first send is
    coming so it is expected rather than filed as spam.

## Close

21. **If this arrives every morning for a month and you only read one section, which is it?**

    That section goes first. Everything else is negotiable, including whether it survives week
    three.
