# The full catalogue

The exhaustive sweep. `SKILL.md` carries the working set; this is for a deep pass, or when a draft still reads wrong after the checklist comes back clean.

**Density is still the rule.** One hit is noise. Three in a passage means rewrite the passage.

---

## Structural

- **Negation reframe** — "not X, it's Y" · "not because X, but because Y" · "you don't need X, you need Y" · "Not A. Not B. The C itself." The dominant tell. Say the positive thing once.
- **Scenario painting** — a claim, then two or three sentences restating it cinematically. Test: can you predict the elaboration from the claim? Then delete it and keep one specific, unpredictable detail.
- **Invented vignette** — "A customer walks into your branch…" · "Imagine a developer who…" · "It's Friday and the finance team…". Opening on a made-up scene to manufacture relatability. The highest-frequency tell in marketing text. Fictional is fictional however specific the details.
- **Meta-commentary on your own structure** — "here's the thing nobody says out loud" · "those are the obvious ones; here's what you don't expect."
- **Announcing implicitness** — "nobody asked for that specifically, it just happened" · "you won't see this in a proposal." Saying a thing is implicit makes it explicit. Demonstrate instead.
- **Rule of three** — "they X, they Y, they Z" · "The A. The B. The C." Delete two.
- **Antithesis couplet** — "Stop keeping up. Start understanding." · "Everyone's using it. Nobody understands it." A slogan shape.
- **Uniform paragraph shape** — every section the same size, entered the same way (claim → elaboration → landing). If paragraphs reorder losslessly, the structure is generic. Vary: some points one sentence, some four.
- **Restating and padding** — said once, said again. Includes closings that summarise the thesis.
- **"Challenges / future outlook" coda** — "Despite its strengths, X faces challenges… but continues to thrive." A canned outline shape.
- **Trailing participle clauses** — "…, reflecting its continued relevance" · "…, creating a lively community" · "…, facilitating the movement of goods." Fact, comma, "-ing" editorial. The most reliable syntactic tell.
- **Questions that assume their answer.** Delete.

## Framing

- **"The hidden cost of X" / "The real cost of X"** — rewrite from a different angle.
- **"Are you a [role] who [problem]?" / "Built for [role] who [problem]"** — conditional gatekeeping.
- **"What your [vendor] won't tell you"** — attacking a third party to borrow authority.
- **"X things you need to know about Y"** — listicle framing.
- **"The biggest risk isn't X, it's Y"** — generic contrarian, and a negation reframe wearing a hat.
- **"What you should [verb]"** — prescriptive. Observe instead.
- **Announcing expertise** — "ten years have taught us" · "as seasoned practitioners."
- **Scare framing** — "what happens if you don't act" · "left behind" · "replaced by it."
- **Manufactured scarcity or urgency** — "seats are limited" · "before it's too late" · "don't miss out."
- **Business-outcome clichés** — "end-to-end visibility" · "seamless operations" · "digital transformation is a journey." If it fits any post on any professional network, it is dead text.
- **Overclaiming** — if it is not proven or specific, delete the claim rather than softening it.
- **Implying the audience lacks capability** — their constraint is usually circumstance, not competence, and readers can tell which one you assumed.
- **Announced significance** — "game-changer" · "pivotal moment" · "marks a turning point."
- **Manufactured broader trend** — "reflects a broader shift" · "part of a movement toward" · "cements its role as." Unearned importance, worst on mundane facts.
- **Notability by assertion** — telling the reader something is important or widely covered instead of showing it: "significant, sustained coverage" · "maintains an active social media presence." Replace with the specific fact.
- **Elegant variation** — renaming the same thing every clause: "the company → the firm → the enterprise → the organisation." Pick one and repeat it.
- **Vague one-liners that fit any industry** — the knowing has to be about something in particular.

## Words and phrases

- **Kill words:** delve, leverage, navigate, elevate, unlock, intricate, synergy, empower, landscape, ecosystem, seamless, robust, game-changer, future-proof.
- **Copula avoidance** (replacing plain *is* / *has*): serves as, stands as, boasts, features, offers, represents, acts as, holds a pivotal place. Restore the plain verb.
- **Inflation verbs:** is a testament to, underscores, highlights, showcases, solidifies, sets the stage for.
- **Significance scaffolding:** marks a pivotal moment, represents a shift, key turning point, plays a vital role in, shaping the future of.
- **Stiff synonym swaps** (the fancy one gets picked): authored (wrote), utilised (used), relocated (moved), attempted (tried), passed away (died), commenced (began).
- **Promotional adjectives:** vibrant, rich heritage, diverse array, breathtaking, nestled, in the heart of, gateway to, showcasing, commitment to.
- **"Active presence" idiom:** "maintains an active social media presence."
- **Didactic disclaimers:** "it's important to note" · "it's worth noting" · "it is crucial to."
- **Vague-authority weasel:** "experts argue" · "observers note" · "researchers say" · "some critics argue" — especially with zero or one source behind it.
- **Non-exhaustive "such as" / "including"** before a list the source never marked open-ended.

## Punctuation and formatting

- **Em dashes, curly quotes, proper ellipses (…)** used unconsciously. People type hyphens, straight quotes and three dots. Deliberate use is fine; saturation is the tell.
- **Inline-header vertical lists** — `- **Bold lead-in:** text` for every bullet. Convert to prose or plain bullets.
- **Emoji as decoration** — 🧠 🎯 📌 ☑️ 👉 leading headings or list items.
- **Bold saturation** — every key term bolded, "key takeaways" styling.
- **Title Case In Headings**, skipped heading levels, a horizontal rule before every heading.
- **Uniform sentence length** throughout.
- **Tiny tables** for three or four facts that belong in a sentence.

## Residue — any one is a hard reject

- **Chat leftovers:** "Certainly!" · "I hope this helps" · "Of course!" · "You're absolutely right!" · "Would you like me to…" · "Here's a template you can customise."
- **Compliance residue:** "As an AI language model…" · "to ensure it aligns with guidelines" · "I cannot, but I can…"
- **Knowledge-gap disclaimers:** "as of my last update" · "while specific details are limited" · "based on available information" — usually followed by "likely".
- **Unfilled placeholders:** `[Your Name]` · `[link here]` · `INSERT_SOURCE_URL`.
- **Tool fingerprints:** `utm_source=chatgpt.com` · `utm_source=openai` · `referrer=grok.com` · `citeturn0search0` · `:contentReference[oaicite:0]` · `【85†L261-269】`.

## The rationalisation table — the long version

Violating the letter here is violating the spirit. If you are thinking any of these, you are about to keep a tell.

| The thought | What is actually true |
|---|---|
| "It's genuinely the best framing" | Everything on this list feels like the best framing. That is the mechanism. |
| "This is observational, not prescriptive" | If you are revealing what the reader should look for, you are lecturing. |
| "The structure serves the content" | Check whether the structure is "not X but Y", "everyone says X", or "the obvious ones, then the surprising ones". Those are templates. |
| "I'm acknowledging it, then moving past it" | Acknowledge-then-set-aside is still positioning. Start where the real content is. |
| "The vivid scenario shows I know the terrain" | If the elaboration is derivable from the claim, it is padding. One real detail beats three invented ones. |
| "The rhythm makes it land" | Triplet cadence is a tell. |
| "The reader needs a signpost" | Write the next point. |
| "The piece needs a conclusion" | It needs to stop. |
| "The product literally does this" | Describe what it does for the reader, not what it is. |
| "I'll fix the wording" | The wording is the symptom. Rewrite the passage. |
